\chapter{Analog-to-Digital Conversion}
\label{c4:ch}

{\large\itshape Reading a voltage as a number, and turning a number back into brightness.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Why ADC: reading continuous voltages instead of a simple high/low.
  \item The 10-bit ADC on the ATmega328P and the linear scaling formula.
  \item Registers: \code{ADMUX}, \code{ADCSRA}, and the combined \code{ADC} result register.
  \item A reusable \code{adc_read()} function.
  \item Worked example: potentiometer-controlled LED brightness via software PWM.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

Until now every input has been a pin that is high or low. This chapter is about the
analog-to-digital converter: what an ADC does, and why its result is a discrete number, not a
continuous value. You will compute the voltage a given ADC reading corresponds to, and vice versa;
configure and read from a chosen ADC channel; and see why the ADC needs its own clock prescaler,
separate from the CPU clock.

Read \secref{c4:sec:adc} first: it is the chapter's core material, and it ends with a worked
example that combines the ADC with the Pin Change Interrupts of Chapter~\ref{c3:ch}. Then work the
exercises in \secref{c4:sec:exercises}. The Reflection and Calculation exercises are answered in
Appendix~\ref{app:answers}; the Code exercises have no printed solution, because the board tells
you whether a program does what the exercise asks.
